## BACKGROUND

Titanium dioxide (TiO2, E171) is a whitening agent used in candy, gum, and processed foods. The EU banned it in food (May 2021) after concluding "genotoxicity cannot be ruled out." The US still allows it. Children have the highest exposure (0.18 mg/kg/day) due to candy and gum consumption.

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) - Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis - has increased 22-72% in young adults (1999-2015). Over 100,000 American youth under age 20 now live with IBD. Mechanistic studies show TiO2 nanoparticles activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, disrupt gut microbiome, and damage intestinal barriers.

This intake criteria identifies plaintiffs with:
- Young-onset IBD (diagnosed ages 15-40)
- High documented childhood TiO2 exposure
- No genetic/familial IBD risk factors
- No confounding IBD triggers (antibiotics, NSAIDs, smoking)

## QUALIFYING INJURY

**The client must have been diagnosed with one of the following conditions:**
- **Crohn's Disease** (diagnosed by colonoscopy + biopsy)
- **Ulcerative Colitis** (diagnosed by colonoscopy + biopsy)

**Diagnosis Requirements:**
- Diagnosed between ages 15-40 (young-onset IBD)
- Diagnosis date: **1/1/2010 or later** (due to limited availability of records)
- Medical records documenting diagnosis (colonoscopy report, pathology report, gastroenterologist notes)

**REQUIRED: Qualifying Treatment (Client must have at least one of the following):**
- Hospitalization for IBD flare
- Surgery (bowel resection, colectomy, fistula repair, abscess drainage, ostomy)
- Biologic medications (Remicade, Humira, Stelara, Entyvio, Xeljanz, Rinvoq, Skyrizi)
- Immunosuppressants (azathioprine, 6-MP, methotrexate)
- Corticosteroids (prednisone, budesonide) for IBD
- Other IBD-specific treatment (exclusive enteral nutrition, fecal transplant)
- **OR Death** (where IBD or IBD complications contributed to death)

**EXCLUSIONS (FOR ALL CASES):**
- Client must not have been diagnosed with IBD prior to 2010
- Client must not be over age 45 at time of diagnosis
- Client must not currently consume TiO2-containing products or plan to consume them in the future
- Client must not have been diagnosed with cancer prior to IBD diagnosis (suggests alternative immunologic issue)

## INTAKE CRITERIA - CROHN'S DISEASE OR ULCERATIVE COLITIS

### **Qualifying Use and Product Exposure:**

**Consistent consumption of identified TiO2-containing products during childhood/adolescence:**

- **Duration:** At least 5 years of regular consumption (3+ times per week)
- **Age window:** Primary exposure between ages 5-18 (developmental period)
- **Product categories:**
  - Candy: Skittles, Starbursts, Mike & Ikes, Jell-O, marshmallows
  - Gum: Trident White, Mentos Freshmint, other whitening gums
  - Frosting/icing: Betty Crocker, Duncan Hines, Pillsbury
  - Other: Processed cheese slices, certain yogurts, cake decorations

**Documentation (at least ONE of the following):**
- Purchase receipts (if available)
- Credit card statements showing purchases at candy stores, grocery
- Loyalty card data (CVS, Walgreens, grocery stores)
- School records (if products were in lunches)
- Affidavit from parent/guardian confirming regular purchase and consumption
- Social media posts/photos showing product consumption
- Dental records noting frequent candy/gum consumption

### **Qualifying Age:**

**Birth year: 1985-2010 (inclusive)**
- These cohorts had peak TiO2 exposure during childhood (1990s-2010s)
- Old enough to have developed IBD (ages 15-40 in 2025)
- Young enough that diagnosis is "young-onset" (not typical adult-onset IBD)

### **Latency Period:**

**The IBD diagnosis must be at least 5 years after first TiO2 product use.**

- Ideally: 10-30 years between childhood exposure and IBD diagnosis
- Minimum acceptable: 5 years (allows for early-onset cases)
- Disqualify if IBD diagnosed within 5 years of first exposure (suggests alternative cause)

**FOR CASES INVOLVING DEATH:**

**EXCLUSIONS:**
- Due to limited availability of records, date of death must be within 10 years of initial intake
- Must be able to provide medical records showing IBD diagnosis and treatment
- If cause of death on death certificate is NOT related to IBD or IBD complications (bowel perforation, sepsis, surgical complications), must provide pathology/autopsy records proving IBD connection

## DISQUALIFYING FACTORS

### **Genetic/Familial IBD Risk:**

**DISQUALIFY if one or more of the following:**

- **First-degree relative with IBD** (parent, sibling with Crohn's or UC)
  - Exception: Siblings diagnosed AFTER plaintiff, if both consumed TiO2 products

- **Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry** with ANY family history of IBD (even distant relatives)
  - Note: Ashkenazi Jews have 2-4x higher IBD risk due to genetic factors

- **Known genetic mutations associated with IBD:**
  - NOD2/CARD15 mutations (Crohn's risk)
  - IL23R mutations
  - ATG16L1 mutations
  - Any confirmed genetic IBD syndrome

- **Family history of other autoimmune diseases** in first-degree relatives:
  - Rheumatoid arthritis
  - Lupus (SLE)
  - Multiple sclerosis
  - Type 1 diabetes
  - Psoriasis/psoriatic arthritis
  - (These suggest genetic autoimmune predisposition)

### **Confounding Medical Factors:**

**DISQUALIFY if diagnosed with or experienced BEFORE IBD diagnosis:**

*Infections that can mimic or trigger IBD:*
- Clostridioides difficile (C. diff) infection
- Intestinal tuberculosis
- Cytomegalovirus (CMV) colitis
- Yersinia, Campylobacter, Salmonella infections (severe/recurrent)

*Other GI conditions:*
- Celiac disease
- Microscopic colitis
- Ischemic colitis
- Radiation enteritis/colitis
- Diverticulitis (chronic/recurrent)

*Immunodeficiency:*
- HIV/AIDS
- Common variable immunodeficiency (CVID)
- Chronic granulomatous disease
- Other primary immunodeficiencies

*Surgical history:*
- Appendectomy BEFORE IBD diagnosis (appendectomy is protective for UC, confounds causation)

### **Medication/Drug Exposures:**

**DISQUALIFY if history of chronic use BEFORE IBD diagnosis:**

- **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin): Chronic daily use (>3 months)
  - NSAIDs can trigger IBD flares and may contribute to IBD development

- **Antibiotics:** >3 courses per year during childhood
  - Antibiotics disrupt microbiome (alternative IBD cause)

- **Oral contraceptives:** Long-term use (>2 years) before diagnosis (weak UC association)

- **Accutane (isotretinoin):** Any use before IBD diagnosis
  - Known to cause IBD-like symptoms

- **Immunosuppressants:** Any use (suggests pre-existing autoimmune condition)

### **Lifestyle/Behavioral Factors:**

**DISQUALIFY if:**

- **Smoking history:** >1 pack-year before IBD diagnosis
  - Smoking is protective for UC but risk factor for Crohn's (confounds causation)

- **Illicit drug use:** IV drug use, cocaine (risk of ischemic colitis, infections)

### **Pregnancy/Birth Complications (if pediatric-onset IBD):**

**DISQUALIFY if plaintiff experienced:**

- Preterm birth (before 37th week of pregnancy)
- Birth weight less than 5.5 lbs
- NICU stay >1 week
- Significant birth trauma or complications

**DISQUALIFY if mother experienced during pregnancy:**

- Substantial cigarette smoking (>5 cigarettes/day)
- Substantial alcohol use (>1 drink/day)
- Any cocaine or illicit drug use
- Diabetes (including gestational diabetes)
- Severe infections (requiring antibiotics)
- Inflammatory bowel disease (mother's IBD creates genetic risk)

### **Geographic/Jurisdictional Restrictions:**

**DISQUALIFY if plaintiff resides in state where minor tolling is not permitted:**
- Connecticut
- Florida
- Indiana
- Louisiana (unless minor is legally disabled)
- New York (unless <6 years since diagnosis)
- [NOTE: Review with legal team for complete list]

**DISQUALIFY if plaintiff resides outside US:**
- Exception: May accept Canadian plaintiffs in border states (cross-border jurisdiction)

### **Litigation Conflicts:**

**DISQUALIFY if:**

- Plaintiff has retained counsel for other IBD-related litigation
- Plaintiff is involved in other food contamination litigation (conflict)
- Plaintiff has signed settlement/release with any defendant in this matter
- Plaintiff filed bankruptcy that discharged potential claims

   - Credit card/bank statements

   - Amazon/online purchase history (if applicable)

3. **Third-party corroboration:**
   - Parent/guardian affidavit
   - Sibling affidavit (if also consumed products)

   - Photos showing consumption of products

   - Tagged locations (candy stores, etc.)

### **Genetic Testing (OPTIONAL - but helpful):**

- Genetic panel for IBD-associated mutations (NOD2, IL23R, ATG16L1)
- If negative → strengthens case (not genetic IBD)
- If positive → likely disqualifies (genetic causation)

### **Biomarker Testing (OPTIONAL - but VERY helpful):**

- **Titanium dioxide tissue analysis** (from colonoscopy biopsy samples)
  - If TiO2 nanoparticles detected in intestinal tissue → smoking gun
  - Requires specialized lab (ICP-MS or similar)
  - Plaintiff may need to undergo colonoscopy specifically for this (if recent biopsy not available)

## STRONGEST PLAINTIFF PROFILE

The ideal plaintiff for bellwether trials:

**Demographics:**
- Age 25-35 (diagnosed in 20s or early 30s)
- Birth year: 1990-2000 (peak TiO2 exposure era)
- No family history of IBD or autoimmune disease
- Non-Ashkenazi Jewish (eliminates genetic confounding)

**Exposure:**
- **Documented high consumption** of 1-2 specific products (e.g., Skittles daily after school)
- Purchase records, photos, or strong parental testimony
- 10+ years of regular consumption (ages 5-18)
- Ideally: TiO2 detected in tissue biopsy (biomarker proof)

**Disease Severity:**
- Diagnosed young (ages 15-25)
- Severe disease requiring surgery (bowel resection, ostomy)
- Multiple hospitalizations
- Failed multiple medications, requires biologics
- Lost significant bowel length or has permanent disability

**Clean Medical History:**
- No smoking (ever)
- No chronic NSAID use
- No frequent antibiotics in childhood
- No C. diff or other infections before IBD diagnosis
- Negative genetic testing (no NOD2, IL23R mutations)
- Full-term birth, normal birth weight, no complications

**Sympathetic Story:**
- Child unknowingly poisoned by candy marketed to kids
- Parents trusted that FDA-approved products were safe
- Now has lifelong disease, surgeries, reduced quality of life
- Can articulate how IBD has impacted education, career, relationships
- Photogenic, articulate, credible witness

**Defendant Link:**
- Consumed 1-2 specific products from identifiable defendants (Skittles = Mars)
- Not scattershot consumption of 50 different products
- Clear causal story: "I ate Skittles every day after school for 10 years, now I have Crohn's"

## CASE TIERS (for intake prioritization)

### **TIER 1 (Bellwether Quality):**
- Meets all qualifying criteria
- Zero disqualifying factors
- TiO2 detected in tissue biopsy (biomarker)
- Severe disease (surgery, ostomy, biologics)
- Strong exposure documentation (receipts, photos, testimony)
- Sympathetic, credible plaintiff
- Ages 25-35, diagnosed 15-30

**Estimated case value:** $500K-$2M
**Priority:** Immediate medical review, expert evaluation

### **TIER 2 (Strong Cases):**
- Meets all qualifying criteria
- Zero or one minor disqualifying factor (e.g., appendectomy, weak family history)
- Moderate to severe disease (hospitalization, biologics)
- Good exposure documentation (affidavit + 1 corroborating source)
- Ages 20-40, diagnosed 15-35

**Estimated case value:** $200K-$800K
**Priority:** Full intake, gather additional documentation

### **TIER 3 (Acceptable Cases):**
- Meets qualifying criteria
- One or two minor disqualifying factors
- Mild to moderate disease (outpatient management, standard medications)
- Adequate exposure documentation (affidavit only)
- Ages 15-45, diagnosed 15-40

**Estimated case value:** $100K-$400K
**Priority:** Provisional intake, may defer until Tier 1/2 exhausted

### **TIER 4 (Weak/Reject):**
- Multiple disqualifying factors
- Mild disease (minimal treatment)
- Poor exposure documentation
- Questionable credibility

**Estimated case value:** $50K-$150K (not worth litigation costs)
**Priority:** Reject or hold pending MDL formation

## CASES TO BE DECLINED

**Immediately disqualify if:**

### **No Qualifying Diagnosis:**
- No IBD diagnosis (only IBS, colitis NOS, diverticulitis, etc.)
- Diagnosed before 2010 (record availability)
- Diagnosed before age 15 or after age 45
- No colonoscopy/biopsy confirmation

### **No Treatment:**
- Diagnosis only, no treatment received
- Only 5-ASA medications (Asacol, Pentasa) - too mild, need biologics/surgery/hospitalization

### **Insufficient TiO2 Exposure:**
- Less than 5 years of consumption
- Less than 3x per week consumption
- No childhood/adolescent exposure (only adult consumption)
- Cannot identify any specific TiO2-containing products

### **Genetic/Familial IBD Risk:**
- First-degree relative with IBD (parent, sibling diagnosed before client)
- Ashkenazi Jewish with any family history of IBD
- Positive genetic testing (NOD2, IL23R, ATG16L1 mutations)
- Multiple family members with autoimmune diseases

### **Confounding Medical Factors:**
- C. diff infection before IBD diagnosis
- Celiac disease
- Prior cancer diagnosis
- Chronic NSAID use (>3 months daily ibuprofen/naproxen)
- Frequent antibiotics in childhood (>3 courses/year)
- Accutane/isotretinoin use before diagnosis
- Appendectomy before diagnosis (UC cases only)
- Immunodeficiency disorders
- Smoking history (>1 pack-year)

### **Latency Issues:**
- IBD diagnosed within 5 years of first TiO2 exposure (too short)

### **Birth/Pregnancy Issues (pediatric-onset):**
- Preterm birth (<37 weeks)
- Low birth weight (<5.5 lbs)
- Mother smoked/drank/used drugs during pregnancy
- NICU stay >1 week

### **Jurisdictional/Litigation:**
- Resides in state without minor tolling (if diagnosed as minor): CT, FL, IN, LA, NY
- Already retained counsel for IBD litigation
- Already signed settlement/release with defendants
- Bankruptcy discharged potential claims

### **Record Availability:**
- Deceased >10 years, no medical records available
- Cannot provide colonoscopy/pathology reports
- Cannot identify treating physician/hospital
