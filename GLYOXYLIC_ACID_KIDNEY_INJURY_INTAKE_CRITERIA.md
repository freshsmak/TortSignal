## BACKGROUND

Glyoxylic acid is a chemical used in hair straightening products marketed as "formaldehyde-free" alternatives to traditional Brazilian Blowouts. After FDA proposed banning formaldehyde in hair straighteners (October 2023), manufacturers pivoted to glyoxylic acid as a replacement.

The problem: Glyoxylic acid penetrates the scalp, converts to oxalate in the body, and causes calcium oxalate crystal deposition in the kidneys, leading to **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)** requiring hospitalization and sometimes dialysis.

**Key Evidence:**
- **NEJM Publication (March 2024):** Case report + animal studies confirming mechanism
- **Israel Ban (2023):** Banned after 26 documented kidney injury cases
- **France Alert (October 2024):** ANSES safety warning after 4 hospitalizations
- **60+ documented cases globally** (Israel, France, Tunisia, Brazil, Argentina, US)

**Current Litigation Status:** ZERO lawsuits filed in US (100% pre-litigation opportunity)

This intake criteria identifies plaintiffs with:
- Acute kidney injury after hair straightening treatment
- Use of glyoxylic acid-containing products
- No pre-existing kidney disease or alternative causes
- Medical documentation of AKI requiring treatment

## QUALIFYING INJURY

**The client must have been diagnosed with one of the following conditions:**
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)** - documented by elevated serum creatinine
- **Acute Oxalate Nephropathy** - kidney injury with calcium oxalate crystals in urine
- **Acute Renal Failure** requiring hospitalization or dialysis

**Diagnosis Requirements:**
- Diagnosis date: **2015-2025** (when glyoxylic acid products were in widespread use)
- Medical records documenting AKI (hospital admission, ER visit, nephrologist consult)
- Lab evidence: Elevated creatinine (>1.5 mg/dL or 50% increase from baseline)
- Temporal relationship: AKI onset within **24-72 hours** after hair straightening treatment

**REQUIRED: Qualifying Treatment/Severity (Client must have at least one):**
- Hospitalization for acute kidney injury
- Emergency room visit for kidney-related symptoms
- Dialysis or renal replacement therapy
- IV fluids for kidney injury treatment
- Medications for AKI (thiamine, pyridoxine, bicarbonate)
- Nephrology consultation
- **OR:** Documented elevated creatinine requiring medical intervention

**EXCLUSIONS (FOR ALL CASES):**
- Client must not have pre-existing chronic kidney disease (CKD) before treatment
- Client must not currently use glyoxylic acid hair products or plan to use them
- AKI must have occurred within 72 hours of hair straightening treatment (not weeks/months later)

**Setting:**
- Professional salon treatment (stylist applied)
- At-home use (purchased online or imported)
- Both salon and at-home qualify

**Exposure Frequency:**
- Even **ONE treatment** can cause AKI (documented in case reports)
- Single exposure sufficient if temporal relationship clear
- Multiple treatments increase risk

**Documentation (at least ONE of the following):**
- Salon receipt showing Brazilian Blowout/Keratin treatment
- Credit card statement for salon visit
- Stylist testimony/affidavit confirming treatment
- Product packaging/labels (if at-home use)
- Social media posts/photos showing treatment
- Appointment records from salon
- Loyalty program data (salon membership)

### **Qualifying Age:**

**No strict age limits, but typical range:**
- Adults ages **18-60** (primary demographic for Brazilian blowouts)
- Broader demographic than Hair Relaxer (not ethnicity-specific)
- All genders (though 78% of users are women)

### **Timing/Latency:**

**Critical: AKI must occur within 24-72 hours of treatment**

- **Typical onset:** 12-48 hours after treatment
- **Maximum window:** 72 hours (3 days)
- **Symptoms:** Nausea, vomiting, back/flank pain, decreased urine output, fatigue
- **Lab confirmation:** Elevated creatinine within 72 hours

**DISQUALIFY if:**
- AKI occurred >7 days after treatment (suggests alternative cause)
- No documented temporal relationship to hair straightening

### **Recovery Pattern:**

**Most cases recover with treatment:**
- Creatinine normalizes within 2-4 weeks with IV fluids
- Some require 2-3 months for full recovery
- Rare cases: Permanent kidney damage or chronic kidney disease

**Documentation of recovery helpful but not required for qualification**

## DISQUALIFYING FACTORS

### **Pre-Existing Kidney Disease:**

**DISQUALIFY if one or more of the following:**

- **Chronic kidney disease (CKD)** diagnosed before hair treatment
  - Any stage (Stage 1-5 CKD)
  - Baseline creatinine >1.3 mg/dL before treatment

- **History of kidney stones** (calcium oxalate or other types)
  - Creates confounding for oxalate nephropathy diagnosis

- **Polycystic kidney disease (PKD)**

- **Diabetic nephropathy** (kidney damage from diabetes)

- **Hypertensive nephropathy** (kidney damage from high blood pressure)

- **Acute kidney injury in the past** (suggests underlying kidney vulnerability)

### **Alternative Causes of AKI:**

**DISQUALIFY if diagnosed with or exposed to BEFORE hair treatment:**

*Nephrotoxic medications:*
- NSAIDs taken in high doses around time of treatment (>1 week of daily ibuprofen/naproxen)
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs (blood pressure medications)
- Diuretics ("water pills")
- Aminoglycoside antibiotics (gentamicin, tobramycin)
- Contrast dye for CT/MRI within 48 hours of treatment

*Infections:*
- Urinary tract infection (UTI) requiring antibiotics
- Pyelonephritis (kidney infection)
- Sepsis or severe systemic infection

*Dehydration/Volume Depletion:*
- Severe dehydration requiring IV fluids (unrelated to hair treatment)
- Vomiting/diarrhea for >24 hours before treatment
- Heat stroke or severe exercise-induced dehydration

*Other Medical Conditions:*
- Rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown releasing myoglobin)
- Tumor lysis syndrome (cancer treatment complication)
- Hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS)
- Ethylene glycol poisoning (antifreeze ingestion)

### **Confounding Factors:**

**DISQUALIFY if:**

- **Diabetes (uncontrolled):** HbA1c >8% increases baseline kidney risk
  - Note: Well-controlled diabetes (HbA1c <7%) acceptable

- **Chronic liver disease:** Affects drug metabolism, confounds oxalate processing

- **Bariatric surgery (gastric bypass):** Increases oxalate absorption (creates alternative explanation)

- **Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD):** Crohn's disease increases oxalate absorption

- **Primary hyperoxaluria:** Genetic disorder causing high oxalate production
  - Rare, but if diagnosed, DISQUALIFY

- **High dietary oxalate intake** coinciding with treatment (spinach, rhubarb, nuts, tea in excessive amounts)
  - Example: Started high-dose spinach smoothie diet same week as treatment

### **Medication Use (Disqualify if heavy use around treatment time):**

- **Vitamin C supplements >1,000 mg/day** (metabolizes to oxalate)
- **Ethylene glycol exposure** (antifreeze) - mimics glyoxylic acid toxicity
- **Chronic NSAID use** (>3 months daily before treatment)

### **Lifestyle/Behavioral Factors:**

**DISQUALIFY if:**

- **Chronic alcohol abuse** (>2 drinks/day for women, >3 for men)
  - Creates confounding kidney stress

- **IV drug use** (risk of nephrotoxicity from adulterants)

### **Geographic/Jurisdictional Restrictions:**

**DISQUALIFY if plaintiff resides in state without viable tort law:**
- Review state-specific statute of limitations for product liability
- Some states may have shorter SOL for acute injuries (2-3 years)

**DISQUALIFY if plaintiff resides outside US:**
- Exception: May consider Canadian plaintiffs if treated with US-sourced products

### **Litigation Conflicts:**

**DISQUALIFY if:**

- Plaintiff has retained counsel for other hair product litigation
- Plaintiff has signed settlement/release with manufacturers
- Plaintiff filed bankruptcy that discharged potential claims
- Plaintiff is involved in criminal case related to product (e.g., product smuggling)

## STRONGEST PLAINTIFF PROFILE

The ideal plaintiff for bellwether trials:

**Demographics:**
- Age 25-45 (prime Brazilian Blowout demographic)
- Female (78% of market, though males also qualify)
- No pre-existing medical conditions
- Active, healthy lifestyle before injury
- Employed (economic damages)

**Exposure:**
- **Single salon treatment** with documented date
- Specific product identified (Brazilian Blowout brand preferred as market leader)
- Receipt showing treatment within 24-48 hours before AKI
- Stylist can testify to product used

**Medical:**
- Clear AKI: Creatinine spike from normal (<1.0 mg/dL) to high (>2.5 mg/dL)
- Hospitalization required (3-7 days)
- Calcium oxalate crystals in urine (smoking gun)
- No alternative causes (no NSAIDs, no dehydration, no infections)
- Full recovery documented (creatinine normalized)

**Injury Severity:**
- Dialysis required (increases damages)
- ICU admission (shows severity)
- Lost work time (economic damages)
- Permanent kidney damage or chronic kidney disease (rare but highest value)

**Documentation:**
- Hospital records complete
- Lab results showing creatinine spike
- Salon receipt with exact date/service
- Photos from social media showing treatment
- No prior kidney problems

**Sympathetic Story:**
- "I went to the salon for a beauty treatment and ended up in the hospital on dialysis"
- "I trusted the salon when they said it was 'formaldehyde-free' and 'safe'"
- "Now I have permanent kidney damage and may need a transplant"
- Can articulate how AKI impacted life (couldn't work, lost income, medical bills)

**Defendant Link:**
- Used specific brand (Brazilian Blowout = GIB, LLC)
- Not generic/unknown product
- Product still being sold in US market
- Clear failure to warn (Israel ban, NEJM publication, no label warning)

## CASE TIERS (for intake prioritization)

### **TIER 1 (Bellwether Quality):**
- Meets all qualifying criteria
- Zero disqualifying factors
- Hospitalization with dialysis or ICU stay
- Calcium oxalate crystals documented in urinalysis
- Specific product identified with receipt
- Clear 24-48 hour timeline
- No pre-existing kidney disease
- Full medical records available

**Estimated case value:** $150K-$500K
**Priority:** Immediate medical review, expert evaluation

### **TIER 2 (Strong Cases):**
- Meets all qualifying criteria
- Zero or one minor disqualifying factor
- Hospitalization (non-ICU) with elevated creatinine
- Good timeline documentation (within 72 hours)
- Salon treatment confirmed (receipt or stylist testimony)
- Recovery documented

**Estimated case value:** $75K-$250K
**Priority:** Full intake, gather additional documentation

### **TIER 3 (Acceptable Cases):**
- Meets qualifying criteria
- One or two minor issues (e.g., mild baseline creatinine elevation, longer timeline)
- ER visit without hospitalization
- Creatinine elevated but <2.0 mg/dL
- Adequate exposure documentation

**Estimated case value:** $25K-$100K
**Priority:** Provisional intake, may defer until Tier 1/2 exhausted

### **TIER 4 (Weak/Reject):**
- Multiple disqualifying factors
- No clear temporal relationship (AKI occurred weeks after treatment)
- Alternative explanations (dehydration, medications)
- Missing medical records

**Estimated case value:** $10K-$50K (not worth litigation costs)
**Priority:** Reject or hold pending further development
