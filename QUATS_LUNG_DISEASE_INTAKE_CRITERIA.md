## BACKGROUND

Quaternary Ammonium Compounds (Quats, including benzalkonium chloride, didecyldimethylammonium chloride) are disinfectant chemicals used in cleaning products. They are the active ingredients in Lysol, Clorox Healthcare products, and most institutional

 disinfectants.

During the COVID-19 pandemic (2020-2022), cleaning product use increased 300-500% in hospitals, schools, and nursing homes. Professional cleaners sprayed Quat-based disinfectants constantly in enclosed spaces, often without proper ventilation or respiratory protection.

**The Problem:**
- Quats are respiratory sensitizers (asthmagens) and irritants
- Cause occupational asthma and COPD in cleaning workers
- No OSHA exposure limits exist (unlike many other chemicals)
- NIOSH issued warnings during COVID but manufacturers didn't update labels

**Key Evidence:**
- **JAMA 2019:** 33% increased COPD risk in nurses using disinfectants weekly (73,262 participants)
- **Multicenter Study 2021:** 2.5% of occupational asthma cases are Quat-induced (confirmed by bronchoprovocation)
- **ECRHS Study 2018:** Cleaners lose lung function equivalent to smoking 10-20 cigarettes/day (6,235 participants, 20-year follow-up)
- **COVID Spike:** 77% increase in human blood Quat levels, 109% increase in inhalation exposures during pandemic

**Current Litigation Status:** Zero lawsuits for Quat-induced lung disease (100% pre-litigation)

This intake criteria identifies plaintiffs with:
- Occupational asthma or COPD from Quat exposure
- Employment as professional cleaner (2020-2025, peak COVID exposure)
- Medical documentation of respiratory disease
- No confounding lung disease causes (smoking, other occupational exposures)

## QUALIFYING INJURY

**The client must have been diagnosed with one or both of the following:**

### **Primary: Occupational Asthma**
- New-onset asthma (no prior asthma history before employment)
- Work-related asthma (symptoms worse at work, improve away from work)
- Diagnosed by physician (pulmonologist, allergist, occupational medicine)
- Positive bronchoprovocation testing to Quats (IDEAL but rare)

### **Secondary: COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)**
- Spirometry showing obstruction (FEV1/FVC <0.70)
- Diagnosed in non-smoker or light smoker (<10 pack-years)
- Developed during or after period of heavy cleaning product use
- Excludes emphysema from alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency

**Diagnosis Requirements:**
- Diagnosis date: **2020-2025** (COVID era exposure spike) OR earlier if long-term cleaner
- Medical records documenting diagnosis (pulmonary function tests, chest X-ray, physician notes)
- Spirometry or bronchoprovocation testing (for asthma/COPD confirmation)

**REQUIRED: Qualifying Treatment (Client must have at least one):**
- Prescription inhalers (albuterol, corticosteroids, bronchodilators)
- Nebulizer treatments
- Hospitalization for asthma attack or COPD exacerbation
- Emergency room visit for breathing difficulty
- Oxygen therapy (for severe COPD)
- Pulmonologist or allergist consultation
- Missed work due to respiratory symptoms

**EXCLUSIONS (FOR ALL CASES):**
- Client must not have been diagnosed with asthma BEFORE becoming a cleaner
- Client must not be current heavy smoker (>10 pack-years disqualifies)
- Client must not currently use Quat products or plan to use them

## INTAKE CRITERIA - OCCUPATIONAL ASTHMA AND COPD

### **Qualifying Occupational Exposure:**

**Employment as professional cleaner in high-exposure setting:**

**Qualifying Job Titles:**
- Janitor / Custodian
- Housekeeping staff (hospitals, hotels, schools)
- Environmental services worker (hospitals, nursing homes)
- School maintenance worker / custodian
- Office building cleaning crew
- Nursing assistant (cleaning duties)
- Health technician (cleaning medical equipment)
- Commercial cleaner (contract cleaning company)
- Residential cleaner (if using institutional-grade products)

**High-Exposure Settings (Prioritize):**
- **Hospitals** (highest Quat use)
- **Nursing homes / Long-term care facilities**
- **Schools** (elementary, middle, high school)
- **Office buildings** (commercial cleaning)
- **Hotels** (housekeeping)
- Universities / colleges (janitorial staff)

**Exposure Intensity:**
- **Daily use** of disinfectants (5+ days/week)
- **Multiple hours per shift** (4-8 hours of cleaning)
- **COVID era (2020-2022):** 300-500% increase in frequency
  - Example: Pre-COVID cleaned once/day, COVID cleaned 4-6 times/day
- **Spray application** (creates aerosols, higher respiratory exposure)
- **Enclosed spaces** (bathrooms, patient rooms, classrooms with poor ventilation)

**Duration of Exposure:**
- **Asthma:** Can develop after months of exposure (faster onset)
  - Minimum: 6 months of daily Quat use
  - Typical: 1-3 years
- **COPD:** Requires years of cumulative exposure
  - Minimum: 5 years of heavy cleaning product use
  - Typical: 10-20 years

### **Qualifying Products (Quat-Containing Disinfectants):**

**Major Brands:**
1. **Lysol** (benzalkonium chloride)
   - Lysol IC Quaternary Disinfectant Cleaner
   - Lysol All-Purpose Cleaner
   - Lysol Disinfectant Spray
2. **Clorox Healthcare** (quaternary formulations)
   - Clorox Healthcare Bleach-Free Disinfectant
   - CloroxPro Quaternary products
3. **Ecolab** (institutional supplier)
   - Various Quat-based hospital disinfectants
4. **Diversey** (institutional supplier)
   - Quat-based surface disinfectants
5. **SC Johnson Professional**
   - Windex Multi-Surface (some formulations)
   - Fantastik All-Purpose Cleaner
6. **Generic "Quat" disinfectants** purchased in bulk

**If client is unsure of product:**
- Ask: "Did you use Lysol or similar disinfectant sprays?"
- "Did you clean with products that smell strongly chemical?"
- "Were you told to spray surfaces constantly during COVID?"
- Check employer purchasing records (hospitals buy in bulk)

### **Documentation Requirements:**

**Employment (at least ONE):**
1. W-2 forms showing employment at hospital/school/facility
2. Pay stubs from cleaning company
3. HR records / employment verification letter
4. Union membership (SEIU, etc.) if applicable
5. Co-worker affidavit confirming client worked as cleaner

**Exposure (at least ONE):**
1. **Employer Safety Data Sheets (SDS)** listing Quat products used
2. **Purchasing records** from facility (subpoena if needed)
3. **Training materials** on disinfectant use (COVID protocols)
4. **Affidavit of use** - Client or supervisor confirms daily Quat use
5. **OSHA complaints** filed about chemical exposure (if any)
6. **Co-worker testimony** about cleaning protocols during COVID

### **Qualifying Age:**

**No strict age limits, but typical range:**
- **Asthma:** Ages 25-55 (working-age adults)
- **COPD:** Ages 50-70 (cumulative exposure over decades)
- Most hospital cleaners average age 40+ (75% of workforce)

### **Timing/Latency:**

**Asthma:**
- Can develop within **6 months to 3 years** of exposure
- "Sensitization period" followed by reactive airways
- Work-related pattern: Symptoms worse Monday-Friday, improve on weekends/vacations

**COPD:**
- Requires **5-20 years** of cumulative exposure
- Accelerated decline in lung function (FEV1 drops 3.9 mL/year faster than non-cleaners)
- Diagnosed typically 50+ due to cumulative damage

### **Work-Related Symptom Pattern (For Asthma):**

**Key diagnostic feature:**
- Symptoms worse at work (wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness)
- Improve away from work (weekends, vacations, sick leave)
- Worsen when using Quat products or entering recently cleaned areas
- May have used personal inhaler at work

## DISQUALIFYING FACTORS

### **Pre-Existing Lung Disease:**

**DISQUALIFY if diagnosed BEFORE cleaning employment:**

- **Childhood asthma** (diagnosed before age 18)
  - Exception: Asthma in remission for 10+ years, then reactivated during cleaning job
- **COPD diagnosed before employment** as cleaner
- **Chronic bronchitis** (before job)
- **Emphysema** from alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (genetic)
- **Cystic fibrosis**
- **Interstitial lung disease** (pulmonary fibrosis)

### **Smoking History:**

**Smoking is the #1 confounding factor for COPD:**

- **Heavy smoking (>10 pack-years):** DISQUALIFY for COPD claims
  - Calculate: Packs/day × years smoked = pack-years
  - Example: 1 pack/day for 15 years = 15 pack-years = DISQUALIFY

- **Light smoking (<10 pack-years):** ACCEPT with caution
  - Must show COPD severity disproportionate to smoking history
  - Example: 5 pack-years but severe COPD at age 45 = occupational component likely

- **Never-smokers:** IDEAL plaintiffs
  - COPD in never-smoker is highly unusual = strongly suggests occupational cause

- **Former smokers (quit >10 years ago):** ACCEPTABLE
  - If quit before cleaning employment began

**FOR ASTHMA:** Smoking is less confounding, but heavy smokers (current >1 pack/day) weaken case

### **Other Occupational Exposures:**

**DISQUALIFY if exposed to OTHER lung hazards:**

- **Silica dust** (construction, sandblasting, stone cutting)
- **Asbestos** (older buildings, insulation work)
- **Coal dust** (mining)
- **Welding fumes** (metal work)
- **Paint fumes** (spray painting, auto body work)
- **Grain dust** (farming, agricultural work)
- **Textile dust** (cotton, flax processing)

**If client worked multiple jobs:**
- Cleaning must be PRIMARY job (40+ hours/week)
- Other exposures must be minimal (<10% of work time)

### **Confounding Medical Conditions:**

**DISQUALIFY if:**

- **Severe allergies** requiring immunotherapy before cleaning job
- **Eczema/atopic dermatitis** (suggests allergic predisposition that confounds occupational asthma)
- **Heart failure** (can mimic COPD symptoms - shortness of breath)
- **Obesity with sleep apnea** (confounds respiratory symptoms)
- **Vocal cord dysfunction** (mimics asthma)

### **Medication/Chemical Exposures:**

**DISQUALIFY if chronic exposure to:**

- **Bleach** (sodium hypochlorite) in equal or greater amounts than Quats
  - Note: Most cleaners use BOTH, but if 90% bleach/10% Quats, attribution is harder
  - Ideally: Quats are PRIMARY or EQUAL exposure

- **Ammonia** (also respiratory irritant)
- **Peracetic acid** (PAA) - similar to Quats, but different chemical (Ecolab OxyCide litigation already exists)

### **Geographic/Litigation Restrictions:**

**DISQUALIFY if:**
- Plaintiff resides outside US (focus on US cases initially)
- Plaintiff already filed workers' comp claim that bars product liability (varies by state)
  - Note: Workers' comp does NOT bar upstream product liability against Clorox/Ecolab
- Plaintiff signed settlement/release with cleaning product manufacturers

## DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

### **Medical Records (REQUIRED):**

1. **Spirometry (Pulmonary Function Test):**
   - For COPD: FEV1/FVC <0.70 (obstruction)
   - For asthma: Reversible obstruction (FEV1 improves >12% with bronchodilator)
   - Serial PFTs showing decline over time (if available)

2. **Chest X-ray or CT scan:**
   - Rules out other lung disease (cancer, fibrosis, TB)
   - May show hyperinflation (COPD) or be normal (asthma)

3. **Physician notes:**
   - Diagnosis of asthma or COPD
   - Occupational history documented (job as cleaner)
   - Smoking history documented
   - Treatment plan (inhalers, medications)

4. **Bronchoprovocation testing (IDEAL for asthma, rarely done):**
   - Positive test to Quat exposure = SMOKING GUN
   - Usually only done in research or specialty occupational medicine clinics

5. **Hospitalization records (if applicable):**
   - ER visits for asthma attacks
   - Admissions for COPD exacerbations

### **Employment/Exposure Documentation:**

1. **Employment verification:**
   - Dates of employment
   - Job title
   - Facility name (hospital, school, office building)

2. **Exposure evidence:**
   - SDS (Safety Data Sheets) from employer listing Quat products
   - Training records on disinfectant use
   - Purchasing/supply records (subpoena if needed)
   - Client affidavit describing daily cleaning tasks
   - Co-worker affidavits

3. **Work pattern correlation (for asthma):**
   - Calendar or diary showing symptoms worse on work days
   - Medical notes documenting "work-aggravated asthma"
   - Peak flow meter readings (lower at work, higher at home)

## STRONGEST PLAINTIFF PROFILE

### **For ASTHMA (Primary Target):**

**Demographics:**
- Age 30-50 (working-age adult)
- Female (50% of cleaners, though males also qualify)
- Immigrant worker (53% Hispanic/Latino, 25% Black)
- Low-wage worker ($13/hour average)

**Employment:**
- Hospital environmental services worker (highest exposure)
- Employed 2018-2025 (includes pre-COVID and COVID era)
- Can document 300-500% increase in cleaning frequency during COVID
- No other occupational exposures

**Medical:**
- Never had asthma before cleaning job
- Developed wheezing/shortness of breath within 1-2 years of employment
- Symptoms clearly worse at work, improve on weekends
- Spirometry showing reversible obstruction or bronchial hyperreactivity
- Never-smoker or former smoker (quit >10 years ago)

**Exposure:**
- Used Lysol or Clorox Healthcare products daily (identifiable brands)
- Spray application in enclosed spaces (bathrooms, patient rooms)
- Minimal ventilation
- No respirator provided (only cloth masks during COVID, which don't filter vapors)

**Documentation:**
- Complete medical records
- Spirometry reports
- Hospital employment records
- SDS showing Quat products
- Co-worker testimony about protocols

**Sympathetic Story:**
- "I cleaned hospital rooms during COVID to keep everyone safe"
- "They told me to spray Lysol constantly, but never gave me proper protection"
- "Now I can't breathe without an inhaler and I'm only 38 years old"
- Economic damages (lost wages, can't work full-time)

### **For COPD (Secondary Target):**

**Demographics:**
- Age 50-65 (long-term cleaner)
- 10-20 years cleaning experience
- Never-smoker or light smoker (<10 pack-years)

**Medical:**
- COPD diagnosed despite minimal smoking
- FEV1 <60% predicted (moderate to severe)
- Requires daily bronchodilators or oxygen
- Lung function decline documented over time

**Causation:**
- ECRHS study: Cleaners lose lung function equivalent to smoking 10-20 cigarettes/day
- Client's COPD severity disproportionate to smoking history
- No other occupational exposures (construction, mining, etc.)

## CASE TIERS

### **TIER 1 (Bellwether Quality):**
- Occupational asthma OR COPD in never-smoker
- Hospital or nursing home cleaner (highest Quat exposure)
- Clear work-related symptom pattern (asthma worse at work)
- Spirometry documenting obstruction
- COVID-era employment (2020-2022 peak exposure)
- Identifiable Quat products (Lysol, Clorox Healthcare)
- Complete medical/employment records

**Estimated case value:** $150K-$400K
**Priority:** Immediate review

### **TIER 2 (Strong Cases):**
- Asthma or COPD with light smoking history (<10 pack-years)
- School or office building cleaner
- Good exposure documentation
- Medical diagnosis confirmed by specialist

**Estimated case value:** $75K-$250K
**Priority:** Full intake

### **TIER 3 (Acceptable Cases):**
- COPD with moderate smoking (10-20 pack-years) but severity disproportionate
- Adequate medical/employment records
- 5-10 years cleaning experience

- Heavy smoker (>20 pack-years)

## PLAINTIFF QUESTIONNAIRE (30 Questions)

1. Full name, DOB, address, contact
2. Current employment status (still cleaning? retired? disabled?)
3. **What is your respiratory diagnosis?** (Asthma / COPD / Chronic bronchitis / Other)
4. **When were you diagnosed?** (Month/Year)
5. **What symptoms do you have?** (Wheezing, shortness of breath, coughing, chest tightness)
6. **What medications/treatments do you use?** (Inhalers, nebulizer, oxygen, etc.)
7. **Have you ever smoked cigarettes?** (Never / Former / Current)
   - If yes: How many packs per day? For how many years? (Calculate pack-years)
8. **Did you have asthma or breathing problems BEFORE working as a cleaner?** (Y/N)
9. **What is/was your job title?** (Janitor, housekeeper, custodian, etc.)
10. **Where did you work?** (Hospital, school, office building, nursing home, etc.)
11. **When did you work there?** (Start date - End date)
12. **How many hours per week did you clean?** (Full-time 40+, Part-time, etc.)
13. **What cleaning products did you use?** (Lysol, Clorox, generic, other - list all)
14. **How often did you use disinfectants?** (Daily, Multiple times per day, Weekly)
15. **Did you spray disinfectants?** (Y/N) - Spray creates aerosols (higher exposure)
16. **Where did you clean?** (Bathrooms, classrooms, patient rooms, hallways, etc.)
17. **Was the area well-ventilated?** (Y/N) - Poor ventilation increases exposure
18. **Were you given a respirator or mask?** (Y/N - cloth masks don't protect from vapors)
19. **Did COVID change your cleaning frequency?** (Y/N - If yes, how much more?)
20. **Are your symptoms worse at work?** (Y/N - KEY for asthma)
21. **Do symptoms improve on weekends/vacations?** (Y/N - KEY for occupational asthma)
22. **Have you had spirometry (breathing test)?** (Y/N - Results?)
23. **Have you seen a pulmonologist or allergist?** (Y/N - Name/contact)
24. **Have you been hospitalized for breathing problems?** (Y/N - How many times?)
25. **Have you missed work due to breathing problems?** (Y/N - How often?)
26. **Do you have other health conditions?** (Heart disease, diabetes, obesity, etc.)
27. **Did you work other jobs involving chemical exposure?** (Construction, painting, welding, etc.)
28. **Have you filed workers' comp for your breathing problems?** (Y/N)
29. **Do you have medical records available?** (Y/N)
30. **Do you have employment records (W-2, pay stubs)?** (Y/N)

## DISCOVERY TARGETS

### **From Manufacturers (Clorox, Ecolab, Lysol/Reckitt Benckiser):**

1. **Safety Testing:**
   - Inhalation toxicity studies on Quats
   - Respiratory sensitization testing
   - Animal studies (mice, rats exposed to aerosolized Quats)

2. **Knowledge of Harm:**
   - Internal memos about NIOSH warnings (2020-2021)
   - Response to JAMA 2019 study (33% COPD risk)
   - Response to multicenter asthma study (2021)
   - Knowledge of occupational asthma cases

3. **Label Warnings:**
   - Why labels only say "use in ventilated areas" (insufficient warning)
   - Why no warning about respiratory sensitization
   - Why no recommendation for respirators (vs. no protection)

4. **COVID-Era Marketing:**
   - Promotional materials during pandemic
   - "Kill 99.9% of viruses" advertising
   - Sales increase during 2020-2022
   - Did they know usage would spike 300-500%?

5. **EPA Registration:**
   - What toxicity data submitted to EPA?
   - What wasn't disclosed?

### **From Employers (Hospitals, Schools):**

1. **Training:** Were workers warned about respiratory risks?
2. **PPE:** What personal protective equipment was provided?
3. **Ventilation:** Were enclosed spaces ventilated properly?
4. **Product Selection:** Why choose Quat products over safer alternatives?
