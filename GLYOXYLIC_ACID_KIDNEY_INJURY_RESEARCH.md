## 1. NEJM STUDY (March/April 2024, not May 16)

**CONFIRMED: Study exists and is published**

Full Citation:
Robert T, Tang E, Kervadec J, Zaworski J, Daudon M, Letavernier E. "Kidney Injury and Hair-Straightening Products Containing Glyoxylic Acid." N Engl J Med. 2024;390:1147-9.
DOI: 10.1056/NEJMc2400528

Published: March/April 2024 (Volume 390, Issue 12)

**Findings:**
- Patient: 26-year-old Tunisian woman, no prior health issues
- Pattern: THREE consecutive episodes of acute kidney injury
  - Episode 1: June 2020
  - Episode 2: April 2021
  - Episode 3: July 2022
- Each episode occurred immediately after hair-straightening treatment
- Product: Cream containing 10% glyoxylic acid (no glycolic acid)

**Clinical Evidence:**
- Urinalyses (day after application): Elongated calcium oxalate monohydrate crystals
- Pattern: Similar to ethylene glycol intoxication
- Mechanism: Glyoxylic acid → penetrates skin → converts to glyoxylate → metabolized to oxalate → calcium oxalate crystals → kidney damage

**Authors & Institutions:**
- Thomas Robert, Ellie Tang, Jennifer Kervadec, Jeremy Zaworski
- Michel Daudon (crystal pathology expert)
- Emmanuel Letavernier (nephrology)
- French institutions (appears to be Paris-based research team)

**Methodology:**
- Case report with recurrent episodes (natural experiment)
- Animal studies conducted (mice)
- Plasma creatinine in mice increased 28 hours after cutaneous application
- Kidney biopsies showing calcium oxalate crystal deposition

**Study Type:** Published correspondence (letter to the editor with experimental validation)

**Authors' Recommendation:**
"In consideration of the potential nephrotoxicity of topical glyoxylic acid, products containing this compound should be avoided and, we would proffer, discontinued from the market."

**Mechanism (Well-Established):**

1. **Absorption:** Glyoxylic acid penetrates skin/scalp
2. **Metabolism:** Converts to glyoxylate in blood
3. **Conversion:** Liver metabolizes glyoxylate → oxalate
4. **Crystallization:** Oxalate + calcium → calcium oxalate crystals
5. **Deposition:** Crystals deposit in kidney tubules
6. **Injury:** Acute oxalate nephropathy → AKI

**Biological Plausibility: STRONG**

Animal studies confirm mechanism:
- Mice exposed to glyoxylic acid cream (cutaneous application)
- Plasma creatinine elevated 28 hours post-exposure
- Kidney biopsies show calcium oxalate crystals
- Dose-dependent relationship

**Symptoms (Acute, within hours):**
- Abdominal pain
- Lower back pain (flank pain)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Elevated creatinine
- Decreased urine output
- Calcium oxalate crystals in urine

**Severity of Outcomes:**

**Dialysis Rates:**
- Variable by severity
- Israeli series: "Several required dialysis"
- Reported case series (13 patients): NONE required hemodialysis
- Severe cases: Renal replacement therapy documented
- One 13-year-old girl required dialysis

**Recovery:**
- MOST PATIENTS RECOVER with supportive care
- Reversible with early intervention
- Example: Creatinine 2.69 mg/dL → 0.87 mg/dL at 3 months

**Permanent Damage:**
- Risk of irreversible renal damage if treatment delayed
- Long-term outcomes: Limited data
- No deaths reported in published literature

**Treatment:**
- IV fluids (primary)
- Thiamine + Pyridoxine (shift metabolism away from oxalate)
- Early treatment may prevent severe injury
- Dialysis in severe cases

**Deaths:**
ZERO deaths documented in published medical literature.
